1. D is strongest. It distinguishes what the software can verify from what the data cannot establish. I09’s audited crossings support a conditional routing decision, not measured EMS improvements. I15’s exhaustive small-instance benchmark can establish bundle-selection correctness without claiming ecological recovery or superiority to SARP. I02 adds a meaningfully different environmental inspection workflow and makes uncertain landfill extent visible. This is the best balance of feasibility, defensibility, and diversity. Its remaining weakness: stable rankings across assumed footprints do not establish that those footprints are plausible.

2. E has the biggest blind spot: confusing available outcomes with identifiable mechanisms. I08’s county-level cause totals cannot establish road-following backhaul topology or distinguish the proposed corridor-failure mechanisms. Scoring only at county level limits the claim’s resolution; it does not repair missing dependencies. E also wrongly dismisses everything without observed outcomes as “opinion”: D’s I15 benchmark can objectively verify an optimization claim. I10’s parcel-age restriction does not by itself supply a historical address baseline or establish that a building was unfindable by 911.

3. All five overlook I07 as a serious alternative. Its core decision is where to collect evidence next, rather than where an inadequately observed hazard supposedly exists. A bounded tributary network, explicit dilution/false-negative assumptions, and comparison against random and coverage-based sampling could demonstrate information-gathering performance. Synthetic injections would validate only the algorithm under those assumptions—not real source attribution. That limitation is explicit and testable. It deserves comparison with I15 on novelty and I02/I04 on investigative usefulness.
