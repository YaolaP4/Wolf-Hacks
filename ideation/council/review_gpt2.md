1. Strongest: D.

D best distinguishes what can be demonstrated from what requires unavailable outcome data. I09 can validate routing and placement under declared blockages without claiming observed EMS improvements. I15 can verify a small-instance optimum without claiming ecological recovery or superiority to SARP. I02 makes footprint uncertainty part of the decision rather than hiding it. These are bounded, actionable projects with different users and credible weekend scopes. A is close, but its I04 recommendation depends on the verified report actually supporting a building-level off-property case; that content is not established here.

2. Biggest blind spot: B.

B treats mergers as improvements without accounting for missing dependencies or implementation cost. Adding I14 cannot repair I08’s unknown backhaul topology, and portable radio relays do not automatically restore cellular service. Combining I12 with I10 compounds uncertain park detection, address matching, and unverified shelter suitability. Combining I15 with I16 does not establish that ecological barrier removal delivers road resilience. These integrations multiply unsupported claims precisely when the team needs one defensible decision.

3. What all five missed:

None seriously evaluates I07. It offers a meaningfully different decision: where to collect evidence next, rather than where to intervene given an assumed map. A small, real stream network could support transparent sample selection, compared against random or coverage-based sampling under varied dilution and false-negative assumptions. Synthetic tests would establish algorithmic behavior—not real-event source identification. That distinction makes I07 a legitimate shortlist challenger, not automatically disqualified by missing historical outcomes.
